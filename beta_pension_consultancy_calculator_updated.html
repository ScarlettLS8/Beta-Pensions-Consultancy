AURORA CARE: A Pension Built Up Year by Year

Aurora CARE builds your pension based on what you earn each year. Each year, you add another slice to your future annual pension. The pension you have already built up then increases under the scheme rules, helping it keep pace with rising prices.

How your Aurora CARE journey works

BASE CAMP · 01

Build Your Base

You contribute 7.5% of pensionable earnings. On £60,000, that is about £4,500 a year or £375 a month.

ASCENT · 02

Accrue Pension

You build annual pension equal to 2.25% of that year's pensionable earnings.

CLIMB · 03

Grow What You’ve Built

While you remain an active member, pension already built up is revalued by CPI + 1.5% each year.

SUMMIT · 04

Retirement Outcome

Your pension is normally payable from Normal Pension Age and increases annually in line with CPI under the supplied scheme information.

What does “career average” mean?

Rather than basing the whole pension only on your final salary, the scheme records pension earned in each year of membership. Each year's pension is added to the pension from previous years, and earlier accrual is increased under the scheme rules. The result is a formula-based pension rather than an individual investment pot.

In plain English: each year adds another building block to your future yearly pension. The blocks you have already earned are adjusted over time under the scheme rules.
Benefits and trade-offs

Advantages and disadvantages

How Aurora CARE may support your goals — and what to weigh up before choosing it.

Advantages

  • More predictable retirement income: benefits are calculated using the scheme formula and are normally payable for life under scheme rules.
  • Less direct investment exposure: your retirement pension is not simply the value of an investment account, which may suit your risk-averse outlook.
  • Specified pension increases: active-member pension is revalued by CPI + 1.5%; pensions in payment increase in line with CPI, according to the supplied information.
  • Family and ill-health protection: scheme information includes a death-in-service lump sum and potential spouse/civil partner, dependant and qualifying ill-health benefits.
  • Continued accrual: each year of membership can add another slice of pension.

Disadvantages

  • Compulsory contributions: about £4,500 a year at your current salary, reducing take-home pay.
  • Early retirement reductions: taking benefits before Normal Pension Age will normally reduce the yearly pension.
  • No automatic retirement lump sum: you may be able to exchange some annual pension for tax-free cash, subject to rules.
  • Normal Pension Age may be later than hoped: it is linked to State Pension Age, with a minimum of 65.
  • Less personal flexibility: there is no individual investment pot for you to direct between funds, and the scheme sets how benefits are calculated.

Family benefits to be aware of

Death in service

The supplied information states a lump sum of twice pensionable earnings if you die while employed and covered by the scheme.

Dependants and ill health

An eligible spouse or civil partner may receive a pension, and qualifying ill-health benefits may be available subject to scheme rules and medical assessment.

Defined Contribution (DC)

Horizon DC: a pension pot you can shape

Horizon DC builds an individual pension pot. Employer contributions, any personal contributions you choose to make, and investment returns affect its value. The pot can rise or fall, and the scheme does not promise a particular retirement income.

How your Horizon DC journey works

BASE CAMP · 01

Build Your Base

At age 45, the employer contribution rate is 13.5%. On £60,000 pensionable salary, this equals £8,100 a year.

ASCENT · 02

Choose Contributions

Your own contributions are optional. If you contribute up to 3%, the employer matches that amount.

CLIMB · 03

Invest Along The Way

Your contributions and employer payments are invested in a fund or funds. Their value can rise or fall.

SUMMIT · 04

Shape Your Outcome

Subject to rules, you may take tax-free cash, buy a guaranteed income, make flexible withdrawals or combine options.

Benefits and trade-offs

Advantages and disadvantages

Advantages

  • Substantial employer contribution: £8,100 a year at your current age and salary, even if you do not contribute personally.
  • Optional personal contributions: you are not required to pay in yourself; contributions up to 3% are matched by the employer.
  • Investment choice: five funds offer different approaches to risk and potential returns.
  • Retirement flexibility: options may include tax-free cash, a guaranteed income, flexible withdrawals or a combination, subject to rules.
  • Pot remains invested if you leave: its future value continues to depend on fund performance.
  • Family protection: the supplied scheme information states account value plus a lump sum of three times pensionable salary if you die before retirement.

Disadvantages

  • Investment performance is uncertain: the pot can rise or fall and could be lower than hoped at retirement.
  • No promised retirement income: the income available depends on the pot, returns, charges and how you use it.
  • More investment responsibility: you need to consider fund choices and your comfort with risk.
  • No guaranteed pension increases: inflation can reduce the purchasing power of your pot and retirement income.
  • Withdrawal and longevity risk: flexible withdrawals that are too high could mean the pot runs out while you still need income.

What if you do not contribute personally?

The employer would still contribute £8,100 a year at your current salary and age, based on the information provided. If you chose to contribute 3% (£1,800), the employer would match that £1,800, making total annual contributions £11,700. The future pot value is not guaranteed and cannot be inferred from contribution totals alone.

Death Benefits

If you die before retirement, the supplied scheme information states that the value of your pension account plus a lump sum of three times your pensionable salary is payable to your beneficiaries, subject to scheme rules.

Leaving Service

If you leave your job, your Horizon DC account remains yours and stays invested. Its value can rise or fall with the performance of your chosen fund or funds until you access it at retirement.

Why not Horizon DC?

Horizon DC has a strong argument in its favour: at your current age and salary, the employer contributes £8,100 a year even if you do not contribute personally. If you contribute 3% (£1,800), the employer matches this amount, giving total annual contributions of £11,700 under the stated rules. It also offers greater investment and retirement flexibility.

However, a high contribution does not guarantee a high or secure retirement income. The pot can rise or fall, there are no guaranteed pension increases, and you would need to manage investment choices and withdrawals.

Fair comparison: Horizon DC remains a credible alternative, especially if keeping take-home pay, investment control or flexible access to savings matters more to you than a more predictable formula-based pension.

How To Read This Estimate

For this illustration, CPI is assumed to average 3.0% a year, so active-member revaluation is 4.5% a year. After retirement, the pension is assumed to increase with CPI at 3.0% a year. Accrual is modelled monthly in arrears rather than as one annual addition. The calculator’s retirement estimate is expressed in annual pension at retirement, not as a cash lump sum or a present-day value.

Plain-English Glossary

Key pension definitions

Choose a term to expand its explanation. Use the search box to find a word or phrase without opening every dropdown.

Retirement and pension basics

CARE — Career Average Revalued Earnings

A workplace pension where you build up pension each year based on your pensionable earnings. Pension already earned is increased over time under the scheme rules.

Defined Benefit (DB) pension

A pension where the amount you receive is worked out using a formula set by the scheme, rather than depending directly on the value of an individual investment pot.

Defined Contribution (DC) pension

A pension where contributions are invested in an individual account. The amount available at retirement depends on contributions, investment performance and charges.

State Pension Age (SPA)

The age at which you can normally start receiving your State Pension from the government. It depends on your date of birth and may change under government rules.

Normal Pension Age (NPA)

The age when your workplace pension is normally payable under the scheme rules. For Aurora CARE, it is linked to State Pension Age, but cannot be lower than 65.

Retirement income

The regular money you receive when you retire. It may come from your workplace pension, the State Pension and other savings.

Accrued pension

The pension you have built up through your membership of a pension scheme so far.

Pensionable service / membership

The time you have been an active member of the pension scheme and building up pension benefits.

How your pension builds up and increases

Pensionable earnings

The parts of your pay used to calculate your pension and contributions. Under Aurora CARE, this includes basic salary and regular taxable pay such as allowances, routine overtime and recurring bonuses.

Pension accrual rate

The rate used to calculate how much pension you build up each year. Under Aurora CARE, you earn pension equal to 2.25% of your pensionable earnings for each year of membership.

Example: if all £60,000 of salary is pensionable, 2.25% is £1,350 of annual pension earned for that year, before revaluation.

CPI — Consumer Prices Index

A measure of how prices for goods and services change over time. It is commonly used to measure inflation and calculate pension increases.

Inflation

The general rise in prices over time. When prices rise, the same amount of money may buy fewer goods and services.

Revaluation

An increase applied to pension already built up, according to scheme rules. Under Aurora CARE, pension earned in earlier years is revalued by CPI + 1.5% while you remain an active member, according to the information supplied.

Contributions, tax and investment

Employee contributions

The money you pay from your earnings into your workplace pension. Under Aurora CARE, contributions are compulsory; under Horizon DC, personal contributions are optional.

Contribution rate

The percentage of pensionable earnings paid into a pension scheme by a member or employer, depending on which contribution is being described.

Tax relief

A tax benefit that can reduce the effective cost of pension contributions. The exact treatment depends on how contributions are collected and your circumstances.

Pension pot

The money accumulated in a defined contribution pension account, including contributions and investment gains or losses, after applicable charges.

Investment risk

The possibility that investments may perform poorly or lose value. With a DC pension, investment performance has a direct effect on the value of your pension pot.

ESG — Environmental, Social and Governance

Environmental, Social and Governance describes factors that may be considered when assessing companies or investments. Environmental factors can include energy use or pollution; social factors can include worker treatment; and governance factors can include how a company is managed. An ESG fund may use these factors to screen or select investments, but the exact approach depends on the fund's policy. ESG does not by itself guarantee stronger investment returns or lower risk.

Tax-free cash

A lump sum that may be taken from a pension without paying income tax on that payment, subject to tax rules and limits.

Lump sum

A one-off payment rather than a regular pension income. Aurora CARE does not automatically provide a retirement lump sum, but you may be able to exchange some pension for tax-free cash, subject to the rules.

Retirement choices, family protection and ill health

Early retirement

Choosing to take your workplace pension before its normal pension age. Your yearly Aurora CARE pension will normally be reduced if you take it early.

Actuarial reduction

A reduction to your yearly pension that may apply if you take it early, because it is expected to be paid for longer.

Death-in-service benefit

A payment or pension benefit that may be provided to eligible beneficiaries if you die while employed and covered by the scheme. The supplied information states Aurora CARE has a lump sum of twice pensionable earnings; Horizon DC has account value plus a lump sum of three times pensionable salary if death occurs before retirement.

Spouse or civil partner's pension

A regular pension that may be paid to an eligible surviving spouse or civil partner after a member dies, subject to scheme rules.

Dependant's benefits

Benefits that may be paid to eligible family members or other dependants, depending on the scheme rules.

Ill-health benefits

Pension benefits that may be available if illness permanently prevents you from doing your job, provided you meet the scheme's conditions and medical assessment requirements.

Medical assessment

An evaluation of your health and ability to work, used to help determine whether you qualify for certain ill-health benefits.
